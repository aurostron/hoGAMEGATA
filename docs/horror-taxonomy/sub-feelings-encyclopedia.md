# The Encyclopedia of Horror Sub-Feelings in Video Games

> The master catalogue of emotional states, psychological triggers, and affective registers that define the horror gaming experience.

---

## Introduction: Why "Horror" is Not a Single Emotion

Saying a game is "scary" is like saying food is "flavorful." It tells you nothing about what you are actually tasting.

A player trapped in a dark, rusted submarine in *Iron Lung* does not feel the same emotion as someone running for their life from the Nemesis in *Resident Evil 3*, or someone slowly piecing together a family tragedy in *The House in Fata Morgana*. 

One is **claustrophobic sensory dread**; the second is **visceral adrenaline panic**; the third is **gothic existential melancholy**.

This encyclopedia catalogs every major sub-feeling and psychological state engineered by horror game developers. Each entry breaks down:
1. **The Psychological Definition:** What is happening in the player's mind.
2. **The Evolutionary / Biological Root:** Why our ancestors evolved this reaction.
3. **The Game Design Mechanics:** How audio, camera, and gameplay evoke it.
4. **Masterwork Examples:** Definitive games that excel in this register.

## Realm 1: Spatial & Atmospheric Dread

### 1. Slow-Burn Dread (Anticipatory Anxiety)
* **Psychological Definition:** The agonizing certainty that something catastrophic is going to happen, paired with complete uncertainty about when or where. Dread is the psychological weight of the wait.
* **Biological Root:** Predator vigilance. When our ancestors entered dense brush where predators were known to hunt, the nervous system remained in a state of sustained low-level alert for hours.
* **Game Design Mechanics:** Extended corridors with no combat, ambient audio that rises slowly in pitch and volume before dropping into dead silence, and doors that take several seconds to open.
* **Masterwork Examples:** *Silent Hill 2*, *Amnesia: The Dark Descent*, *PT*.

### 2. Liminal Disorientation (The In-Between)
* **Psychological Definition:** An unsettling feeling of being trapped inside a transitional space that should be full of people, but is completely empty, endless, and stripped of purpose.
* **Biological Root:** Spatial mapping failure. The human brain relies on landmarks to navigate. Endless repeating patterns (yellow wallpaper, fluorescent hallways, empty airport terminals) trigger cognitive distress because our navigational orientation breaks down.
* **Game Design Mechanics:** Non-Euclidean geometry, endless repeating hallways, humming fluorescent lights, empty office cubicles, and surreal architectural loops.
* **Masterwork Examples:** *The Complex: Found Footage*, *Anemoiapolis*, *The Stanley Parable* (Ultra Deluxe dark endings), *Superliminal*.

### 3. Claustrophobia & Entombment
* **Psychological Definition:** The suffocating terror of being compressed into a tight, inescapable physical boundary where movement is restricted and air is finite.
* **Biological Root:** Cave collapse, suffocation, and entrapment where physical struggle is useless.
* **Game Design Mechanics:** Narrow camera FOV (field of view), slow crawling through ductwork or rusted ship hulls, scraping metallic audio, screen borders that darken and close in on the player.
* **Masterwork Examples:** *Iron Lung*, *Amnesia: The Bunker*, *Dead Space* (mining shafts), *Alien: Isolation* (air ducts).

### 4. Thalassophobia (The Abyssal Deep)
* **Psychological Definition:** The overwhelming dread of vast, deep, dark bodies of water—the realization that an immense, pitch-black void exists beneath your feet, and anything could be rising from it.
* **Biological Root:** Terrestrial species vulnerability. In open water, humans have zero mobility, zero visibility, and no terrain to stand on.
* **Game Design Mechanics:** Muffled underwater acoustics, sonar pings, sudden depth drop-offs where the seabed disappears into blackness, colossal silhouettes moving in the distance.
* **Masterwork Examples:** *Subnautica*, *SOMA* (the ocean floor trench), *Sunless Sea*, *Barotrauma*.

### 5. Acoustic Pareidolia (Auditory Haunting)
* **Psychological Definition:** When the brain mistakes harmless ambient noise (wind, pipe creaks, distant hums) for voices, footsteps, or breathing.
* **Biological Root:** Hyper-vigilant acoustic threat detection. In evolutionary terms, a false positive (thinking the wind was a tiger) is harmless, but a false negative (thinking a tiger was the wind) is fatal.
* **Game Design Mechanics:** Infrasound (low-frequency sound waves around 19Hz that induce anxiety and eye vibration), asymmetric binaural audio, and subtle footsteps placed just outside the player's peripheral hearing.
* **Masterwork Examples:** *Darkwood*, *Hellblade: Senua's Sacrifice*, *Phasmophobia*.

## Realm 2: The Uncanny & Cognitive Shock

### 6. The Uncanny Valley (The Almost-Human)
* **Psychological Definition:** A profound sense of revulsion and distrust triggered by something that looks almost human, but is subtly artificial, dead, or corrupted.
* **Biological Root:** Evolutionary pathogen and corpse avoidance. An entity that looks human but moves stiffly, has dead eyes, or displays asymmetrical facial movement triggers ancient alarms against rabies, neurological contagion, or decaying cadavers.
* **Game Design Mechanics:** Stiff ragdoll animations, mannequins that reposition when you look away, porcelain doll heads, porcelain smiles that never blink, and human voices played slightly out of sync with lip movements.
* **Masterwork Examples:** *Condemned: Criminal Origins* (Department Store mannequins), *Little Nightmares*, *Resident Evil Village* (House Beneviento), *Signalis*.

### 7. Analog Decay & Corrupted Media
* **Psychological Definition:** Dread evoked by low-fidelity, degraded technology—VHS magnetic tape distortion, public access emergency broadcasts, scanlines, degraded cassette audio, and glitched CRT monitors.
* **Biological Root:** Corrupted signals. We rely on clear sensory data to assess reality. When the transmission is corrupted, our mind senses that whatever corrupted the machine is now in the room with us.
* **Game Design Mechanics:** Screen tearing, color bleeding, faux-found-footage camcorder perspectives, synthetic text-to-speech audio, and degraded 1980s government warning sirens.
* **Masterwork Examples:** *Signalis*, *Fears to Fathom*, *The Mortuary Assistant*, *No Players Online*, *The Backrooms: Lost Tape*.

### 8. Masquerade of Innocence (Mascot Horror)
* **Psychological Definition:** The terrifying subversion of childhood comfort objects—theme park mascots, cartoon characters, nursery rhymes, toys, and playgrounds—twisted into bloodthirsty, predatory entities.
* **Biological Root:** Violation of safe harbor. Childhood spaces are biologically wired as zones of parental protection. Corrupting these spaces shatters the primal feeling of security.
* **Game Design Mechanics:** Upbeat carnival music slowed down and played out of tune, oversized furry suits with exposed mechanical teeth, bloodstains on pastel wallpaper.
* **Masterwork Examples:** *Five Nights at Freddy's*, *Poppy Playtime*, *Bendy and the Ink Machine*, *Doki Doki Literature Club!*.

### 9. Paranoia & Perceptual Gaslighting
* **Psychological Definition:** When the game makes the player doubt their own senses, sanity, or memory.
* **Biological Root:** Cognitive collapse. The fundamental horror of knowing your sensory hardware can no longer be trusted.
* **Game Design Mechanics:** Items subtly changing position when you revisit a room, doors locking and unlocking without sound, portraits whose eyes track the cursor only on the edge of the screen, and fake inventory glitches.
* **Masterwork Examples:** *Eternal Darkness: Sanity's Requiem*, *Silent Hill 4: The Room*, *Layers of Fear*, *Milk inside a bag of milk*.

## Realm 3: Biological & Visceral Revulsion

### 10. Body Betrayal & Mutation
* **Psychological Definition:** The visceral horror of your own body (or the human form) turning against you through uncontrolled cellular growth, mechanical grafting, bone distortion, or grotesque transformation.
* **Biological Root:** Somatic integrity defense. The horror of physical mutilation and cancerous corruption.
* **Game Design Mechanics:** Cracking bone audio, flesh ripping, characters being forced to cut off limbs to survive, grotesque hybrid anatomy.
* **Masterwork Examples:** *Dead Space*, *The Last of Us Part II* (The Rat King), *Resident Evil 2 Remake* (William Birkin), *The Thing*, *Scorn*.

### 11. Parasitic Violation & Contagion
* **Psychological Definition:** The skin-crawling panic of foreign organisms burrowing under your skin, taking control of your nervous system, or consuming you from within.
* **Biological Root:** Parasite and insect evasion. The sensation of itching and crawling triggers reflexive shuddering and frantic grooming.
* **Game Design Mechanics:** Audio of insects buzzing inside walls, visible movement under the protagonist's skin, status effects requiring surgical extraction or cauterization.
* **Masterwork Examples:** *Resident Evil 7: Biohazard* (Marguerite Baker), *Inside*, *Carrion*, *Alien: Isolation* (Facehuggers).

### 12. Motor Frailty & Physical Sluggishness
* **Psychological Definition:** The nightmare sensation of needing to run, but your legs feel like lead; needing to scream, but no sound comes out.
* **Biological Root:** Sleep paralysis and exhaustion in the face of a predator.
* **Game Design Mechanics:** Heavy stamina bars that leave the player wheezing, limping after taking damage, tank controls that make turning slow and deliberate, weapons with heavy recoil and slow reload animations.
* **Masterwork Examples:** *Resident Evil Classic*, *Darkwood*, *Cry of Fear*, *Silent Hill 1*.

## Realm 4: Psychological & Existential Terror

### 13. Cosmic Dread (Lovecraftian Insignificance)
* **Psychological Definition:** The staggering realization that humanity, Earth, and all our struggles are an inconsequential speck in a vast, cold universe ruled by ancient, indifferent, god-like entities who do not even notice our existence.
* **Biological Root:** Loss of purpose and existential vertigo. Humans survive by believing our actions matter. Cosmic horror destroys that delusion.
* **Game Design Mechanics:** Unknowable colossal geometries, eldritch deities whose true form drains sanity, texts written in incomprehensible alien scripts, futility endings where victory is impossible.
* **Masterwork Examples:** *Bloodborne*, *World of Horror*, *Call of Cthulhu: Dark Corners of the Earth*, *SOMA*, *Returnal*.

### 14. Guilt Manifestation & Karmic Persecution
* **Psychological Definition:** The horror of being trapped in a purgatory created by your own moral failings, repressed memories, or unforgivable crimes. The monsters outside are reflections of the evil inside.
* **Biological Root:** Social shame, moral exile, and conscience.
* **Game Design Mechanics:** Symbolism embedded in monster designs (e.g. Pyramid Head representing punishment and sexual guilt), environments that recreate crime scenes or hospital rooms from the protagonist's past.
* **Masterwork Examples:** *Silent Hill 2*, *Cry of Fear*, *Omori*, *The Cat Lady*.

### 15. Existential Grief & Melancholic Decay
* **Psychological Definition:** A quiet, hollow dread born from profound loss, bereavement, and watching everything you love decay into nothingness.
* **Biological Root:** Mourning and depression.
* **Game Design Mechanics:** Faded sepia/monochrome palettes, lonely classical piano scores, diaries of dead families, empty children's rooms.
* **Masterwork Examples:** *The House in Fata Morgana*, *What Remains of Edith Finch*, *Signalis*, *Detention*.

### 16. Absolute Solitude & Abandonment
* **Psychological Definition:** The cold realization that no one is coming to save you. You are the last survivor, stranded on a dying space station, a forgotten bunker, or a dead planet.
* **Biological Root:** Tribal separation. For ancient humans, being cast out of the tribe was a death sentence.
* **Game Design Mechanics:** Radio chatter going dead one by one, discovering the bodies of your team members who were relying on you, total absence of friendly NPCs.
* **Masterwork Examples:** *SOMA*, *Alien: Isolation*, *System Shock 2*, *Dead Space*, *Lone Survivor*.

## Realm 5: Survival & Adrenaline Panic

### 17. Stalker Pursuit Panic (The Relentless Hunter)
* **Psychological Definition:** The adrenaline-fueled terror of being hunted by an unkillable, intelligent pursuer who never stops searching for you.
* **Biological Root:** Apex predator pursuit. Knowing you cannot fight back; your only option is evasion and survival.
* **Game Design Mechanics:** Unscripted AI roaming the entire map, heavy footsteps echoing in nearby rooms, dynamic music that swells when line-of-sight is broken, door handles rattling.
* **Masterwork Examples:** *Alien: Isolation* (The Xenomorph), *Resident Evil 2 Remake* (Mr. X), *Resident Evil 3* (Nemesis), *Outlast* (Chris Walker), *Clock Tower* (Scissorman).

### 18. Scarcity & Inventory Agony
* **Psychological Definition:** The mounting panic of entering a dangerous zone knowing your resources are critically low. Every bullet spent is a future death warrant.
* **Biological Root:** Winter starvation and resource depletion.
* **Game Design Mechanics:** Strict grid-based inventory systems, combining herbs to make medicine, manual typewriter ribbons required to save progress, scarce ammunition.
* **Masterwork Examples:** *Resident Evil 1 & 2*, *Tormented Souls*, *Signalis*, *Darkwood*.

### 19. Startle Reflex (The Jump Scare)
* **Psychological Definition:** A primitive, involuntary motor reaction to a sudden, violent sensory change (a screeching stinger, glass shattering, a figure leaping from darkness).
* **Biological Root:** Acoustic startle reflex. Evolutionary survival mechanism designed to make animals flinch away from sudden strikes.
* **Game Design Mechanics:** Strategic lull in audio followed by a high-decibel acoustic spike and rapid screen movement. (When overused, it becomes cheap; when properly set up with dread, it is devastating).
* **Masterwork Examples:** *Dead Space*, *Outlast*, *Five Nights at Freddy's*, *Resident Evil 1* (Lickers through two-way mirror).

## Realm 6: Relational & Social Horror

### 20. Yandere & Obsessive Stalking
* **Psychological Definition:** The terror of suffocating, violent romantic obsession—where an entity loves you so intensely they will imprison, isolate, or mutilate you so no one else can have you.
* **Biological Root:** Intrasexual aggression, entrapment, and loss of relationship autonomy.
* **Game Design Mechanics:** Loving letters that turn violently possessive, smiling characters with bloodstained knives, dialogue choices where every option traps you further.
* **Masterwork Examples:** *Doki Doki Literature Club!* (Monika), *The Freak Circus*, *Misao*, *Yandere Simulator*, *You and Me and Her*.

### 21. Mob Paranoia & Folk Isolation
* **Psychological Definition:** The dread of being an outsider in an isolated rural community where everyone is in on a sinister ancient tradition, cult, or blood sacrifice.
* **Biological Root:** Out-group hostility and xenophobia. The horror of being outnumbered in unfamiliar territory.
* **Game Design Mechanics:** Townsfolk speaking in hostile dialects, torchlight processions in the distance, altars made of animal bones in basements.
* **Masterwork Examples:** *Resident Evil 4* (El Pueblo), *Outlast 2*, *Mundaun*, *Bloodborne* (Central Yharnam), *Silent Hill* (The Order).
